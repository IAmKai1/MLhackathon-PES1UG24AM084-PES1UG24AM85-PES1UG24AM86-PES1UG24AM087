Raw GPS traces are noisy: buildings, multipath reflections and dropouts make a vehicle appear on a parallel street or jump across a block. PathSnap reconstructs the road segments a vehicle actually travelled by matching every 1 Hz GPS observation to an arc of the trip's road network.

The pipeline has four stages. For each observation we generate up to ten nearby candidate arcs. A classifier scores each candidate with the probability that it is the reference arc, using 19 geometric, directional and contextual features. A Hidden Markov Model decoded with the Viterbi algorithm then combines these scores with road-network connectivity, so the final route is physically drivable. The trained model and decoder are served by a FastAPI backend on one laptop and queried by a browser-based evaluator interface on a second laptop.

We compared seven classifiers from seven algorithm families using trip-grouped cross-validation and selected HistGradientBoosting, which we then tuned with Bayesian optimisation. On the ten student-testing trips, HMM decoding raised observation-level accuracy from 0.908 (classifier only) to 0.956 and route continuity from 0.941 to 0.979. On the ten evaluator-testing trips, run once through the frozen pipeline, accuracy rose from 0.914 to 0.957, which shows the system generalises to unseen trips.
