HMM decoding clearly improves the reconstructed route. On the ten student-testing trips, observation-level accuracy rose from 0.908 (classifier only) to 0.956, route continuity from 0.941 to 0.979, and route-set F1 from 0.900 to 0.936. All ten trips improved. Only two trips had any chain breaks (two in trip 0, one in trip 56), and decoding took 0.1 to 1.0 s per trip.

The largest gain was trip 77, from 0.793 to 0.901. Its classifier-only route contains many short excursions onto side streets, visible in the route-comparison figure, which the connectivity constraint removes. Trips 9, 56, 76 and 91 each gained four to five points. The smallest gains were on trips that were already easy (trip 7: 0.986 to 0.996) and on trip 65 (0.863 to 0.879). In trip 65 the classifier's remaining errors are mostly shared by the HMM (82 observations wrong under both methods), which suggests nearby arcs that are all reachable from one another, so the connectivity constraint gives the decoder no basis to prefer the reference arc.

The error breakdown shows the HMM is not uniformly beneficial. Of 19,013 scored observations, it fixed 1,095 that the classifier had wrong, but broke 167 that the classifier had right, a net gain of 928. A likely cause of broken observations is the decoder briefly preferring a connected neighbouring arc to the correct one, because switching to the correct arc would cost an unlikely transition. 661 observations remained wrong under both methods, concentrated in trips 77 (321), 65 (82) and 9 (62). Candidate misses explain only a handful of these, since the reference arc was missing from the candidate set for 0.08% of student-testing observations. Most are therefore cases where both methods prefer a nearby arc, such as a parallel road or an adjacent arc at a junction, or where the derived label itself is uncertain.

The same frozen pipeline was run once on the ten evaluator-testing trips: accuracy rose from 0.914 to 0.957 and continuity from 0.934 to 0.964, with no chain breaks. The near-identical student-testing and evaluator accuracies (0.956 and 0.957) indicate that neither the classifier nor the decoder is overfitted to the development data.
