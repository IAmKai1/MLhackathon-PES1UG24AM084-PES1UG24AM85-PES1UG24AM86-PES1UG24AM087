Our primary selection metric is observation-level Top-1 accuracy on the student-testing trips, because it measures exactly what the system must deliver: the correct road for each GPS point. We also required good probability quality (low log loss), since the HMM uses the classifier's probabilities directly as emission scores, and fast inference for the API.

HistGradientBoosting meets all three. It has the highest student-testing Top-1 accuracy (0.9026), the highest student-testing macro F1 (0.9386) and the lowest log loss (0.0715), at 0.004 ms per observation. RandomForest has a marginally higher cross-validation macro F1 (0.9393 vs 0.9382), but the difference is far smaller than the fold-to-fold standard deviation (about 0.014), and RandomForest is weaker on the student-testing trips (Top-1 0.8914), overfits more and is nine times larger on disk (27 MB vs 3 MB). The fold-level Top-1 scores show both models are stable: HistGradientBoosting ranges from 0.878 to 0.932 across folds, and the ranking between folds is driven by which trips each fold contains rather than by the model.

The overfitting table supports the choice. The gap between training and cross-validation macro F1 is small for HistGradientBoosting (0.020) and for the linear and neural models, but large for RandomForest (0.037) and KNN (0.034), whose training scores are inflated because they can memorise individual training rows.
